Where the data comes from, and why goal difference isn't a problem

Built from real results, not a guess. Two open archives feed it: footballcsv/cache.footballdata (a mirror of football-data.co.uk, ~30 countries, 1993–2024) and seanelvidge/England-football-results (English top-flight results back to 1888, including the era before 1981 when a win was worth 2 points rather than 3). The dropdown picks which of those trains the model.

Goal difference's official tie-break rule has actually changed over the game's history — English football used goal average (goals scored ÷ goals conceded) before 1976, then switched to goal difference (scored − conceded) after. That history doesn't get in the way here, though: goal difference isn't being used to settle who officially finishes above whom (that's still handled by real final points), only as a second, independent signal alongside the points gap — computed the same way, scored minus conceded, in every season regardless of what the era's own tiebreak rule was. Both gaps are fed into one joint logistic regression per scenario × data-source combination, so the model can learn things like "level on points but well ahead on goal difference" as its own distinct, real, historically-grounded situation.

To keep the page light, points gaps are grouped in bands of 2 and goal-difference gaps in bands of 4 before fitting — finer than that made barely any difference to the fitted curve but roughly doubled the amount of data shipped to your browser.

The shaded band and "likely range" still come from a Wilson score interval on the nearest matching real bin, exactly as before — it collapses to nothing once the points gap alone is mathematically decided, since goal difference is irrelevant if a team can't be caught on points at all.

The 3×games remaining mathematical ceiling from before still applies, based on points alone: goal difference only ever matters as a second-order signal or a genuine tiebreaker, never as something that can mathematically overturn a settled points gap.

Sources: footballcsv/cache.footballdata & seanelvidge/England-football-results (GitHub). Illustrative regression, not a live forecast for any current league.
