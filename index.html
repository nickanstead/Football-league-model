Where the data comes from, and what "previous season" adds

Built from real results, not a guess. Two open archives feed it: footballcsv/cache.footballdata (a mirror of football-data.co.uk, ~30 countries, 1993–2024) and seanelvidge/England-football-results (English top-flight results back to 1888, including the era before 1981 when a win was worth 2 points rather than 3). The dropdown picks which of those trains the model.

Previous-season finishing position was tested before being added, not just assumed to help: fitting on the first 80% of English top-flight seasons chronologically and checking the held-out most recent 20% showed a consistent improvement — a team's prior finish adds real signal even after you already know their current points and goal difference, most likely because it's a rough proxy for underlying squad quality that a few weeks of this season's results haven't fully revealed yet.

It was originally built as a margin — your previous finish compared to the cutoff team's. A follow-up test found that was the wrong shape: your own previous finish carries 10 to 50 times more weight in the fitted model than the cutoff team's does, so their history was doing almost nothing. Dropping it and using only your team's own previous-season position, on its own, matched the two-team version's accuracy exactly for two of the three scenarios and lost only a negligible amount on the third — which is what's now deployed: one honest slider instead of an implied, largely irrelevant comparison.

A team that wasn't in the top flight the previous season (freshly promoted, or simply not matched by name) is treated as if it finished below the bottom of the previous table — a deliberate simplification, since tracking second-tier form would need a different, and for the free data source, less accessible, dataset.

Worth being precise about what the slider's positions actually mean: 2nd through 20th are real previous top-flight finishes — including the low ones, which mostly describe a team that narrowly avoided relegation rather than one that came up from below. "Promoted" is a separate, single bucket sitting below all of them, not a continuation of the 18th–20th end of the scale. Right now it doesn't distinguish an automatically-promoted side (usually the stronger one) from a team that scraped up via the play-offs, or even which position they actually finished in the second tier — every promoted team gets the same flat placeholder. That's not because the data doesn't exist: the same archive contains full second-tier results, which could in principle place a promoted team on this same scale using their actual Championship finish rather than a single stand-in value. That refinement hasn't been built or tested, so it isn't reflected here yet.

All three gaps — points, goal difference, and previous-season finish — are fed into one joint logistic regression per scenario × data-source combination, fit offline rather than in your browser (the model is the same either way; doing it offline just keeps this page light). What still runs live here is the mathematical certainty check and the uncertainty band.

That uncertainty band uses its own separate lookup table, and it used to round points into bands of 2 and goal difference into bands of 4 to stay small — which meant "1 point behind" and "exactly level" were silently treated as the same situation, even though a leader who only needs a draw plays very differently from one who's already been drawn level with. That's now fixed: gaps of 6 points or less, and 8 goals or less, keep their exact value; only gaps bigger than that (where the mathematical certainty rule is usually already doing the real work anyway) still get rounded. The trade-off is that a genuinely exact tie is rare in real football history, so pinpointing it precisely often means a very small, very wide-ranging sample — which the band will now show honestly instead of hiding it inside a falsely reassuring bigger number.

The 3×games remaining mathematical ceiling still applies, based on points alone: neither goal difference, previous-season form, nor games in hand can mathematically overturn a settled points gap — though a game in hand does correctly widen how many games remain to close it.

Games in hand were added the same way as everything else here: tested before being built, not assumed to matter. A held-out chronological test found the current model barely reacted to games in hand at all for realistic gaps — a real blind spot, since anyone watching a title race discounts a "4 points behind" table position that comes with 2 games in hand. Adding it as its own input, with its own interaction with time of season (a game in hand is worth far more with 4 games left than with 30), gave a consistent, statistically real improvement across all three scenarios, using genuine historical fixture pile-ups rather than a synthetic assumption.
